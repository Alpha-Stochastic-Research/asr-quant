\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Motivation and Problem}
Quantitative research is often fragmented across tools that solve individual tasks well but leave the researcher responsible for preserving the links between them. A data series may describe period $t$ yet only become available at $t+1$; a curve can calibrate numerically while failing to reproduce its input quotes; a strategy can look strong after selection from many trials; a portfolio can appear robust before costs, capacity, or scenario sensitivity are considered; and a backtest can become difficult to reproduce once its data, seed, configuration, and implementation assumptions are separated from the result.

\pkg\ treats this fragmentation as a research-architecture problem. The goal is not to replace specialized market-data systems, pricing libraries, execution platforms, or institutional governance. The goal is to provide a coherent layer in which research inputs, hypotheses, market conventions, quantitative methods, validation evidence, decisions, and artifacts can be inspected together.

Version 1.3.0 extends the 1.2 canonical API without replacing it. The release concentrates on infrastructure: market conventions and schedules, curve diagnostics and quote-space risk, credit foundations, scenario analysis, point-in-time data, strategy-selection diagnostics, experiment lineage, reproducible reports, extensible adapters, and stronger public-API compatibility controls.

\subsection{Research Question}
The central question is:
\begin{quote}\itshape
Can a unified quantitative-finance platform connect hypothesis discovery to market infrastructure, model validation, portfolio decisions, and backtesting while preserving chronology, provenance, and implementation semantics?
\end{quote}

The evaluation therefore asks whether the platform can: (i) distinguish what a datum describes from when it became usable; (ii) calibrate and diagnose market structures rather than merely return an optimizer status; (iii) expose model and strategy-selection risk; (iv) retain reproducible experiment and artifact lineage; and (v) preserve earlier public interfaces while adding these controls.

\section{Related Literature and Positioning}
\subsection{Quantitative Methods}
The platform composes established quantitative methods rather than claiming novelty for their underlying mathematics. Portfolio construction connects to mean--variance analysis \cite{markowitz1952}; performance evaluation requires care under serial dependence and non-normality \cite{sharpe1966,lo2002}; derivative and rate modules build on classical no-arbitrage models \cite{blackscholes1973,merton1973,vasicek1977,cir1985,heston1993}; robust covariance and time-series inference draw on standard econometric tools \cite{neweywest1987,englegranger1987,ledoitwolf2004}.

The 1.3 validation layer is motivated by the risk of selecting strategies after trying many alternatives. Reality Check, SPA, Deflated Sharpe, PBO, and multiple-testing evidence motivate an explicit distinction between exploration and confirmation \cite{white2000,hansen2005,bailey2014,bailey2017,harvey2016}. The software therefore treats statistical evidence as one component of a research decision rather than as an automatic strategy approval.

\subsection{Architecture Positioning}
The differentiating object is the \emph{research chain}. ASRQuant is designed so that a team can use its own data provider, pricing engine, or cost model while retaining common experiment, validation, provenance, and reporting contracts. This reduces the incentive to force every institution onto a single infrastructure stack and makes interoperability a first-class requirement.

\section{Data, Point-in-Time Information and Provenance}
\subsection{Data Sources and Quality}
The canonical data layer continues to accept pandas objects, local tabular files, public HTTP(S) tables, and provider-backed data including Yahoo Finance, ECB, FRED, Binance, and Alpha Vantage. Validation reports missingness, duplicate indices, non-finite values, constant columns, ordering, and sampling gaps without silently rewriting the input.

Version 1.3 adds deterministic snapshots and a point-in-time contract. Let $t_o$ denote the observation timestamp, $t_a$ the first timestamp at which the value was available to the researcher, and $t_r$ a later revision timestamp. A valid research view at decision time $t$ may use an observation only if $t_a \leq t$. This prevents the common error of treating revised historical data as if the revised value had been known in real time.

\paperfig{figures_clean/figure_03_point_in_time.png}{Point-in-time data separates observation time, availability time, and revision time. An \texttt{as\_of} view includes only information available by the decision timestamp.}

\subsection{Snapshots and Experiment Identity}
A data snapshot stores a deterministic fingerprint, source metadata, schema, creation time, and serialized representation. A local \texttt{DataStore} adds keyed caching, freshness checks, and offline replay of previously cached data. An experiment combines named configuration, data fingerprints, a seed, optional code hash, package/Python version, and environment metadata. The resulting fingerprint can be registered in an append-only local registry and used as a stable reference for downstream artifacts. \texttt{ResearchReport} can combine heterogeneous result objects into HTML, Markdown, or JSON. If an upstream artifact changes, a research graph identifies dependent artifacts that should be considered stale.

\begin{lstlisting}
snapshot = store.put("rates_panel", data, source="internal")
experiment = asr.research.Experiment(
    name="curve_signal",
    config={"lookback": 60, "cost_bps": 5},
    data={"market": data}, seed=42,
)
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Hypothesis Discovery and Identification}
\subsection{Evidence and Novelty}
The hypothesis system introduced in earlier releases remains intact. Candidates can arise from data, literature, model disagreement, robustness instability, structural changes, regimes, or domain-specific observations. The core rule remains conservative: a statistically supported candidate is not automatically novel, and a corpus-relative research gap is not proof of global novelty.

Evidence status and novelty status are therefore represented separately. This separation lets a candidate be out-of-sample supported yet already documented in prior work, or potentially novel while still statistically inconclusive.

\paperfig{figures_clean/figure_04_hypotheses.png}{Illustrative candidate states showing statistical evidence and novelty as separate dimensions.}

\subsection{Failure Conditions}
A candidate should be weakened or rejected when its sign changes materially across reasonable specifications, its holdout evidence disappears, a chronology correction removes the effect, multiple-testing adjustment eliminates the finding, an alternative benchmark explains the result, or prior literature establishes that the purported mechanism is already known. The platform records these outcomes rather than forcing all candidates toward a positive decision.

\section{Market Infrastructure}
\subsection{Conventions, Schedules and Instruments}
Version 1.3 adds a common conventions layer for TARGET, US federal, UK bank, and weekend-only calendars; custom holidays; business-day adjustments; day counts; stubs; frequencies; fixing/payment lags; accrual fractions; and schedules. These are not cosmetic details: they determine accrual fractions, cash-flow dates, discounting intervals, and hedge sensitivities. The high-level instrument layer covers deposits, FRAs, fixed-rate bonds, swaps, and OIS objects with explicit pricing, cash-flow, and risk methods, so that rate workflows do not rely on ad hoc date arithmetic in each notebook.

\subsection{Curve Construction and Diagnostics}
A curve build is treated as a constrained numerical object with explicit diagnostics. Given market quotes $q$ and internal curve parameters $z$, the builder retains the market quotes and returns a curve, quote-repricing errors, calibration diagnostics, and a quote-to-zero Jacobian $\partial z/\partial q$. Sparse swap maturities can use an explicit \texttt{grid\_policy=interpolate}, with synthetic coupon-grid nodes recorded in metadata, while \texttt{grid\_policy=strict} requires a completely supplied coupon grid. The test fixture used in this paper combines short deposits and par swaps. The calibrated curve reproduces the input quotes to numerical tolerance while preserving positive discount factors.

\paperfig{figures_clean/figure_05_curve_builder.png}{Deterministic 1.3 curve-builder example. The displayed maximum quote-repricing error is numerical rather than economic.}

\subsection{Quote-Space Risk and P\&L Explain}
Risk is often consumed in market-quote space rather than in an arbitrary internal zero-rate grid. By the chain rule,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial V}{\partial q}=\frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial q}.
\end{equation}
ASRQuant exposes node risk and quote risk separately. For a par swap, the deterministic example concentrates most quote PV01 in the terminal swap quote, as expected from the calibration structure of the sparse fixture.

\paperfig{figures_clean/figure_06_quote_risk.png}{Quote-space PV01 for an illustrative 5Y par swap calibrated to the deterministic market-quote set.}

Rate P\&L explanation then compares instrument values under two curve states and attributes the move using the same explicit curve objects. This provides a consistent path from market inputs to valuation, sensitivities, scenarios, and subsequent review.

\section{Credit, Scenarios and Cross-Domain Risk}
\subsection{Credit Foundations}
Version 1.3 introduces piecewise-constant hazard and survival curves together with default probabilities, CDS premium/protection-leg analytics, par spreads, CS01-style hazard sensitivity, jump-to-default diagnostics, and sequential hazard bootstrapping. For piecewise hazard rate $\lambda(t)$, survival is
\begin{equation}
Q(0,T)=\exp\left(-\int_0^T \lambda(u)\,du\right).
\end{equation}
The deterministic example bootstraps a hazard curve from 1Y, 3Y, and 5Y CDS spreads under a fixed recovery assumption. The purpose is infrastructure validation, not an empirical credit forecast.

\paperfig{figures_clean/figure_07_credit.png}{Illustrative survival probabilities implied by the deterministic CDS bootstrap fixture.}

\subsection{Scenario Engine}
A common scenario object can represent parallel, slope, curvature, volatility, liquidity, or asset shocks and route them to compatible instruments or portfolios. This allows scenario assumptions to be named, stored, compared, and attached to research reports instead of remaining embedded in isolated notebook cells.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Research Utilities}
The 1.3 utility layer also includes generic calibration with residual, Jacobian, and conditioning diagnostics; first-, second-, and cross-sensitivity estimation; fixed, spread, linear-impact, and square-root-impact cost models; EVT tail modelling, filtered historical simulation, and drawdown-at-risk; Gaussian and Student-$t$ copulas; factor and Brinson attribution; volatility and structural-break regime diagnostics; model and covariance-estimator comparison; Monte Carlo variance-reduction helpers; rate P\&L explanation; signal-portfolio capacity diagnostics; and adapter protocols for data, pricing, risk, execution, cost, and optimizer components.

\section{Research Validation}
\subsection{Selection Risk and Chronology}
A strategy that is selected from many alternatives has a different evidential status from a strategy specified before looking at the data. ASRQuant 1.3 therefore adds combinatorial purged cross-validation, PBO, Reality Check, SPA, leakage diagnostics, and consolidated strategy reports. These diagnostics complement rather than replace economic reasoning.

\paperfig{figures_clean/figure_08_validation_diagnostics.png}{Deterministic synthetic example showing PBO, Reality Check, and SPA diagnostics. The values demonstrate API behavior only.}

\subsection{Multiverse and Leakage Diagnostics}
Multiverse analysis evaluates a research conclusion across multiple reasonable choices such as lookback, normalization, neutralization, cost assumptions, or model specification. Leakage diagnostics search for observable chronology violations including future shifts, unsafe train/test overlap, and transformations that use information outside the training window. Neither tool proves validity; both make fragility easier to identify before a result is promoted.

\section{Portfolio, Costs and Backtesting}
\subsection{Decision Layer}
The existing portfolio layer remains available for minimum variance, maximum Sharpe, risk budgeting, maximum diversification, and hierarchical risk parity, with 1.3 additions for constrained/cost-aware optimization, robust mean--variance construction, risk budgeting, and covariance-estimator comparison. Fixed, spread, linear-impact, and square-root-impact transaction-cost models and scenario objects can be passed into the same decision workflow, reducing the gap between an unconstrained research optimum and an implementable allocation.

\subsection{Backtest as Implementation Contract}
The backtesting layer continues to require explicit chronology. Let $w_t$ be effective asset weights, $r_{t+1}$ next-period returns, and $C_{t+1}$ implementation cost. A minimal net return identity is
\begin{equation}
r^{net}_{t+1}=w_t^\top r_{t+1}-C_{t+1}.
\end{equation}
The important property is not the formula itself but that delay, turnover, costs, cash, borrow, and missing-data assumptions are part of the recorded implementation rather than post-hoc adjustments.

\paperfig{figures_clean/figure_09_backtest.png}{Deterministic synthetic illustration showing how explicit costs change the backtest path.}

\section{Experimental Design and Release Validation}
\subsection{Validation Design}
The paper evaluates software behavior, not a market anomaly. Tests therefore use deterministic fixtures, fixed random seeds, mocked provider responses, known pricing identities, and controlled synthetic data. Numerical controls include quote repricing, discount-factor/zero-rate round trips, par-swap zero-PV identity, CDS bootstrap repricing, Black--Scholes put--call parity, finite-difference sensitivities, positive-semidefinite covariance checks, and deterministic experiment fingerprints.

The review re-execution collected 336 tests across 36 modules and completed with 336 passes and no pytest skips. Tests were run in isolated groups because plotting, solver, and numerical backends can otherwise share process state. Combining the grouped coverage data gives 77.36\% statement coverage across \texttt{src/asrquant} (13,092 statements; 2,964 missed). Coverage is reported as scope evidence rather than as a correctness score.

The package import sweep reports 84 package modules imported with zero failures, excluding \path{asrquant.__main__} because importing it intentionally executes the CLI. A stable API snapshot tracks 116 declared symbols, while a separate signature inventory records 258 top-level public callables; automated compatibility checks fail when a stable symbol disappears or its callable signature changes unexpectedly. Canonical result serialization is native in the final artifact. \section{Release-Candidate Review Corrections and Numerical Integrity}
\subsection{Why the release-candidate review matters}
A numerical library can pass a large internal test suite and still carry a localised convention error. The final 1.3.0 release therefore treats the release-candidate review as a separate validation workstream. That workstream was led by co-author Srijan Mishra and covered numerical correctness, statistical conventions, public interfaces, reproducibility, edge cases, defect reproduction, regression protection and release-readiness evidence. It was organizationally internal to ASR, not third-party independent model validation. Separation here means that identified defects were reproduced independently from the implementation change and challenged through explicit tests; it does not imply institutional independence. A finding is incorporated only when its reproduction identifies a concrete contract, convention, numerical boundary, or dependency behaviour. Statistical estimation limits are not relabelled as software defects merely because their estimates can be noisy.

\subsection{Key-rate DV01 on uneven market pillars}
The most material rates finding concerned triangular key-rate bumps. A symmetric triangle built from the larger neighbouring spacing is harmless on uniform grids but overlaps on an irregular market pillar set. If $w_i(t)$ denotes the bump weight associated with pillar $i$, a first-order partition requires
\begin{equation}
\sum_i w_i(t)=1
\end{equation}
through the supported curve grid. The final implementation uses the actual left and right gaps separately. On the standard uneven pillar set $[0.25,0.5,1,2,3,5,7,10,15,20]$, this removes the spurious risk spillover that could otherwise make key-rate DV01s fail to reconcile with a parallel DV01. Automatic key-rate bumps now also require the selected maturity to coincide with a curve pillar; arbitrary centres remain available only when the caller supplies an explicit width.

\subsection{Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe Ratios}
The Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio is defined on a per-observation Sharpe statistic. Feeding an annualised Sharpe into the same finite-sample formula mixes scales with the benchmark and makes the multiple-testing deflation too weak. The final implementation retains the public annualisation parameter for compatibility but computes the PSR statistic from the per-observation Sharpe. DSR then compares like with like. This correction is especially important because the result is a probability-like quantity: a scale error can look plausible rather than fail loudly.

\subsection{Non-identifiable implied volatility}
When a Black-style option quote lies at the discounted intrinsic boundary, floating-point arithmetic can make the objective exactly zero at the solver's lower volatility bracket. Returning that lower bracket creates a false impression of a successful calibration. The final implementation explicitly evaluates the bracket boundary and refuses the inversion when the quote is at the non-identifiable boundary. Interior quotes still use bracketed root finding and preserve the existing round-trip behavior.

\subsection{Stable PCA orientation, statistics compatibility and explicit conventions}
Principal-component eigenvectors are defined only up to sign. The final yield-curve PCA orients each component using its largest absolute loading, preventing arbitrary sign inversions from contaminating rolling factor interpretation while leaving reconstruction unchanged. Removed statsmodels 0.15 keywords are no longer passed to AutoReg or Granger-causality functions. RSI-14 now defaults to Wilder smoothing, while the alternate simple-moving-average convention remains explicitly selectable. Gaussian-process observation noise is fixed when supplied by the user and the requested and fitted values are both reported. Vasicek calibration now reports standard errors for the AR(1) coefficient and the implied mean-reversion speed, making the well-known short-sample uncertainty visible rather than pretending that calendar-span limitations can be solved by denser sampling.

\subsection{Dependency architecture}
The final core installation is smaller than the RC dependency surface. Matplotlib/Plotly/Jinja2, scikit-learn, requests, and pypdf are behind explicit extras and are imported only when their capabilities are invoked. A fresh \texttt{import asrquant} therefore does not itself load Matplotlib, requests, or scikit-learn. The purpose is not merely import speed: regulated, offline, or minimal environments can audit a smaller dependency graph and add specialised stacks intentionally.

\section{Native Quantitative Engines and Numerical Independence}
ASRQuant keeps its finance-specific model implementations inside the package rather than delegating pricing, curve construction, rate risk, calibration or derivative analytics to an external quantitative-finance engine. This design makes model equations, conventions and numerical boundaries inspectable in the same source tree as the research workflow that uses them.

Independence does not mean avoiding the scientific Python ecosystem. NumPy, SciPy, pandas and statsmodels provide general numerical, array, data and econometric primitives where appropriate. The distinction is that these libraries do not substitute a separate finance-pricing engine for ASRQuant's own instrument and risk logic.

Validation therefore emphasizes model-specific mathematical contracts. European option routines are checked by put--call parity, implied-volatility round trips and an independently coded Black--Scholes formula; curve and rate-risk routines are checked by repricing and partition identities where applicable; stochastic and statistical routines are checked against known moments, invariants, deterministic seeds, limiting cases and regression fixtures.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Primary Result}
The main result is architectural rather than predictive. ASRQuant 1.3.0 can represent a continuous research path from point-in-time inputs and hypothesis candidates through market calibration, validation, scenarios, portfolio decisions, and auditable backtesting while recording the provenance and dependencies of intermediate artifacts.

\section{Robustness, Limitations and Implementation Boundaries}
\subsection{Robustness Controls}
The 1.3 tests exercise both normal and failure paths, including sparse/complete curve grids, randomized option parity, credit repricing, multiverse execution, point-in-time revisions, portfolio constraints, cost positivity, Monte Carlo variance reduction, regime diagnostics, and artifact-lineage propagation. API compatibility and distribution-install checks protect a different failure mode: a mathematically correct implementation that is difficult to install or unexpectedly breaks existing notebooks.

\subsection{Limitations}
The release is not a replacement for venue-specific holiday databases, institutional entitlements, a complete credit/XVA stack, exchange execution infrastructure, or independent model-validation governance. The point-in-time contract can only be as reliable as the availability metadata supplied to it. Multiple-testing diagnostics depend on the candidate set presented to the test. Curve and credit examples in this manuscript are simplified deterministic fixtures. Scenario and cost models are modelling assumptions, not observed future execution conditions. Guarded live-broker components remain separate from software-release status and are designed to fail closed.

The current local evidence is platform-specific: Linux x86\_64 and Python 3.13.5. Cross-platform package publication should therefore preserve the hosted compatibility matrix as a separate release gate; the manuscript does not report that gate as locally observed.

\section{Economic and Research Implications}
For a researcher, the practical benefit is fewer silent translations between stages. For a quantitative team, the adapter layer means internal providers, pricers, or desk cost models can be registered without abandoning the common research workflow. For review, fingerprints and lineage make it easier to answer which data, configuration, code version, and seed generated an artifact. For model governance, diagnostics create explicit places to record inconclusive, unstable, or failed results rather than forcing every workflow into a successful numeric output.

The intended standard is therefore not feature count. A capability belongs in the platform when it improves correctness, traceability, validation, interoperability, or decision relevance without weakening the stable public API.

\section{Mathematical and Implementation Identities}
\subsection{Quote-space sensitivity}
If an instrument value $V$ depends on internal curve nodes $z$ and those nodes depend on market quotes $q$, first-order quote sensitivity is
\begin{equation}
\nabla_qV=(\nabla_zV)\,\frac{\partial z}{\partial q}.
\end{equation}
The distinction matters because a portfolio is usually hedged and reported against traded quotes rather than an arbitrary interpolation grid.

\subsection{Credit survival}
For piecewise-constant hazard rate $\lambda_k$ on intervals $(t_{k-1},t_k]$,
\begin{equation}
Q(0,T)=\exp\left(-\sum_k \lambda_k\Delta t_k\right),
\end{equation}
with intervals truncated at $T$. The bootstrap chooses hazard segments so the CDS premium and protection legs reproduce the supplied market spreads under the stated recovery convention.

\subsection{Backtest net-return identity}
With effective risky weights $w_t$, next-period asset returns $r_{t+1}$, residual cash return $r^f_{t+1}$, and implementation cost $C_{t+1}$,
\begin{equation}
r^{net}_{p,t+1}=w_t^\top r_{t+1}+\left(1-\mathbf{1}^\top w_t\right)r^f_{t+1}-C_{t+1}.
\end{equation}
This identity makes residual cash and explicit implementation cost part of the recorded backtest contract.

\section{Selected Numerical Contracts}
\subsection{Key-rate partition}
For pillar $t_i$ with neighbours $t_{i-1}$ and $t_{i+1}$, the corrected triangular weight is piecewise asymmetric:
\begin{equation}
w_i(t)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{t-t_{i-1}}{t_i-t_{i-1}}, & t\in[t_{i-1},t_i],\\[1.5mm]
\dfrac{t_{i+1}-t}{t_{i+1}-t_i}, & t\in[t_i,t_{i+1}],\\[1.5mm]
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The left and right distances are not replaced by their maximum. This is the local condition needed for neighbouring tents to form a partition over an irregular grid.

\subsection{PCA orientation}
If $v_j$ is a principal loading vector and $k=\arg\max_i|v_{ij}|$, the release applies
\begin{equation}
\tilde v_j=\operatorname{sign}(v_{kj})v_j,
\end{equation}
with a positive anchor. Scores receive the same sign, preserving reconstruction exactly while producing a stable reporting orientation.

\subsection{Vasicek uncertainty}
With the discretised AR(1) representation $r_{t+1}=a+\phi r_t+\varepsilon_{t+1}$ and $\kappa=-\log(\phi)/\Delta t$, the reported delta-method approximation is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{se}(\kappa)\approx \frac{\operatorname{se}(\phi)}{\phi\Delta t}.
\end{equation}
This diagnostic does not eliminate finite-sample bias; it makes the local identification uncertainty observable to the user.
